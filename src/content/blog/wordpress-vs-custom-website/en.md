It's the first question almost every client asks us: *should we build on WordPress, or go custom?* Both are good answers — for different situations. Choosing the wrong one usually doesn't break a project on day one; it shows up a year later as a site that's slow, expensive to change, or hard for your team to update.

This guide walks through how we actually make that call with clients, without the jargon.

## What "WordPress" and "custom" really mean

**WordPress** is a content management system (CMS). It powers a large share of the web — more than 40% of all websites, according to W3Techs. You get an admin panel out of the box, thousands of plugins, and a huge pool of developers who know it. A *custom WordPress* site means a theme designed and coded for you, not a bought template.

**A custom-built website** is written from scratch with a modern framework such as React or Next.js. Content is either kept in code, pulled from a headless CMS (Sanity, Strapi, ReadyCMS…), or comes from your own database. Nothing is there unless you need it.

The difference isn't "cheap vs premium". It's **ready-made building blocks vs a tailored structure**.

## A quick comparison

| | WordPress (custom theme) | Custom-built (e.g. Next.js) |
|---|---|---|
| Time to launch | Faster for content sites | Longer for the first version |
| Editing content | Familiar admin, very easy | Depends on the CMS you pair it with |
| Performance | Good with care, plugins can slow it down | Excellent by default |
| Unusual features | Plugins, or custom plugin work | Built exactly as needed |
| Security & updates | Regular core/plugin updates needed | Smaller attack surface, fewer moving parts |
| Long-term cost | Low entry, ongoing maintenance | Higher entry, lower friction to evolve |

## When WordPress is the right choice

WordPress shines when **content is the product** and your team wants to publish without calling a developer:

- company and presentation sites with many pages, news or a blog
- sites where marketing needs landing pages, forms and SEO plugins on its own
- online stores on **WooCommerce** with a standard checkout flow
- multilingual sites — WPML or Polylang handle translations well
- projects that need to launch quickly on a predictable budget

The key word is *custom theme*. A lightweight theme built for your design, with only the plugins you really need, gives you most of the speed of a custom build while keeping the editing comfort WordPress is known for. That's the approach behind our [WordPress package](/packages#package-1).

## When a custom build pays off

A custom build makes sense when the website behaves more like **software** than a brochure:

- client portals, dashboards, booking or calculation tools
- CRMs, ERPs and internal tools that connect to other systems via APIs
- products where performance and Core Web Vitals directly affect revenue
- interfaces with heavy animation or 3D that a theme would fight against
- anything where a plugin would be bent so far that it becomes the problem

In these cases, a framework like Next.js lets us ship exactly the features you need, render every page as fast static HTML, and grow the product without fighting the platform. See how we approach [web applications](/packages#package-2).

## Four questions that usually decide it

1. **Who updates the site, and how often?** A non-technical team publishing weekly → WordPress. Rare content changes, mostly features → custom.
2. **Is it a website or an application?** If users log in, calculate, book or manage data, lean custom.
3. **How much does speed matter to the business?** Both can be fast, but custom gets there with less ongoing effort.
4. **What's the 2–3 year plan?** If you already know about integrations, automation or a mobile app, choose the foundation that won't need replacing.

## The hybrid option

It isn't always either/or. Two hybrids we use often:

- **Headless WordPress** — editors keep the WordPress admin, while the public site is a fast Next.js front end.
- **Custom site + headless CMS** — a modern front end with a simple editing panel for the content that changes.

These give you the editing comfort of a CMS and the performance of a custom build, at the cost of a bit more setup.

## Our recommendation

If you're a service business, a manufacturer or a local brand that mainly needs a fast, well-structured site your team can update — **a custom WordPress theme** is usually the best value. If you're building a product, a portal or anything with real logic behind it — **go custom** from the start; migrating later costs more than doing it right once.
