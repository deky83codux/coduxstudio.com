"We should be using AI" is now a sentence we hear in almost every first meeting. Usually followed by: *"…but where do we even start?"* The honest answer is that AI agents are neither magic nor a fad. For a small business they're a practical tool for one thing above all: **taking repetitive, rule-based work off your team's plate.**

Here's what that looks like in real life — and where it doesn't work.

## What an AI agent is (and isn't)

A chatbot answers questions. An **AI agent** can also *act*: read an email, look something up in your CRM, fill in a form, draft a reply, create a task, and hand the result to a person for approval. Under the hood it's a language model connected to your tools through APIs, plus clear rules about what it may and may not do.

What an agent isn't: an employee you can leave unsupervised with your bank account. Good agents are narrow — one job, well defined, with a human checking the output where mistakes would be expensive.

## Five tasks agents handle well in a small company

1. **Inbox triage.** Sort incoming emails and form submissions into *sales*, *support*, *invoices* and *spam*, pull out the key details and route them to the right person.
2. **Lead qualification.** Read a new enquiry, enrich it with public company info, score it against your ideal client and draft a first reply for a salesperson to send.
3. **Answers from your own documents.** Price lists, policies, technical specs — an agent can answer staff or customer questions from *your* documents and link to the source, instead of guessing.
4. **Moving data between systems.** Copying order data from the web shop to the invoicing tool, or from a spreadsheet into the CRM, is exactly the kind of work an agent does reliably once the rules are clear.
5. **Reports and summaries.** A Monday-morning summary of last week's sales, open tickets or project status, written in plain language from the data you already have.

Notice the pattern: each task is **frequent, repetitive and easy to check**. That's the sweet spot.

## Where AI agents fail

- **Vague jobs.** "Handle customer service" is not a task. "Answer delivery-time questions using this table" is.
- **No source of truth.** If your prices live in three spreadsheets that disagree, an agent will confidently pick the wrong one.
- **High-stakes decisions without review.** Payments, legal answers, anything medical — keep a human in the loop.
- **Messy processes.** Automating chaos just produces faster chaos. Fix the process first, then automate it.

Language models can also produce answers that sound right but aren't. That's why good agent design limits *what* the agent can answer, makes it cite sources and escalates to a person when it's unsure.

## Data and privacy

If you work with EU or Serbian clients, personal data falls under GDPR and Serbia's Law on Personal Data Protection. Before connecting an agent to your inbox or CRM, know what data it sees, where it's processed, how long it's kept and who can access the logs. Choose providers that don't train on your data and give you a data processing agreement. It's not complicated, but it has to be decided up front — not after launch.

## How to start without wasting money

1. **List your repetitive tasks.** Ask the team: what do you copy, paste, sort or rewrite every week?
2. **Pick one.** The best first project is boring, frequent and easy to measure — e.g. "sorting 200 enquiries a month".
3. **Run a pilot.** Two to four weeks, with a person approving every output. Measure time saved and the error rate.
4. **Then scale.** Only once the pilot works do you remove the manual review where it's safe, and move to the next task.
